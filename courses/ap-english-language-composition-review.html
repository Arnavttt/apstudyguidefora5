Essential Concepts

  • Rhetorical situation: speaker, occasion, audience, purpose, subject, tone
  • Argument structure: claim, evidence, warrant, backing, rebuttal
  • Modes of appeal: logos (logic), ethos (credibility), pathos (emotion)
  • Style and syntax: sentence variety, figurative language, diction choices
  • Source integration and citation in synthesis writing

Exam Day Tips

✓

For rhetorical analysis, analyze specific choices (not just 'the author uses pathos')—name the device, quote it, explain its effect.

✓

Synthesis essays require citing at least 3 of the 6–7 sources—don't summarize sources, use them as evidence.

✓

Argument essays: take a clear, debatable stance in the first paragraph; don't hedge.

✓

Time management: spend ~5 min planning each essay and ~5 min reviewing.
