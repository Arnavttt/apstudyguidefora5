Practice Questions

Multiple Choice (20 Questions)

Q1. Which colonial policy allowed American colonists to largely govern themselves before 1763?

  • A. Mercantilism
  • B. Salutary neglect
  • C. Virtual representation
  • D. Indentured servitude
Show Answer

Answer: B — Salutary neglect was Britain's unofficial policy of loose enforcement of parliamentary laws, fostering colonial self-governance.

Q2. The primary purpose of the Articles of Confederation was to:

  • A. Establish a strong central government
  • B. Protect states' rights against federal overreach
  • C. Create a bicameral legislature
  • D. Grant Congress power to tax
Show Answer

Answer: B — The Articles deliberately created a weak central government to protect state sovereignty, reflecting colonial fears of tyranny.

Q3. The Missouri Compromise of 1820 admitted Missouri as a slave state and Maine as a free state while:

  • A. Abolishing slavery north of 36°30'
  • B. Prohibiting new slave states north of 36°30' in the Louisiana Territory
  • C. Granting Congress power to regulate slavery in existing states
  • D. Repealing the Three-Fifths Compromise
Show Answer

Answer: B — The compromise drew a line at 36°30' N — slavery prohibited in new states north of that line in the Louisiana Territory.

Q4. Which Supreme Court case ruled that Congress had no authority to ban slavery in territories?

  • A. Marbury v. Madison
  • B. McCulloch v. Maryland
  • C. Dred Scott v. Sandford
  • D. Plessy v. Ferguson
Show Answer

Answer: C — Dred Scott (1857) held enslaved people were property, not citizens, and Congress lacked power to prohibit slavery in territories.

Q5. The Homestead Act of 1862 most directly encouraged:

  • A. Industrial growth in northern cities
  • B. Western migration and agricultural settlement
  • C. Formation of labor unions
  • D. Native American assimilation
Show Answer

Answer: B — The act granted 160 acres of public land to settlers who farmed it for five years, spurring mass westward migration.

Q6. The primary goal of the Sherman Antitrust Act (1890) was to:

  • A. Regulate railroad rates
  • B. Break up monopolies restraining trade
  • C. Establish the Federal Reserve
  • D. Protect labor union organizing
Show Answer

Answer: B — The Sherman Act declared illegal every contract, combination, or conspiracy in restraint of trade, targeting industrial monopolies.

Q7. Which factor most significantly contributed to the U.S. decision to declare war on Spain in 1898?

  • A. Spain's territorial ambitions in Canada
  • B. Yellow press sensationalism and the USS Maine explosion
  • C. German pressure on the Monroe Doctrine
  • D. Congressional desire for tariff revenue from Cuba
Show Answer

Answer: B — Yellow journalism inflamed public opinion and the mysterious Maine explosion gave Congress a pretext for war.

Q8. Booker T. Washington's Atlanta Compromise (1895) argued Black Americans should prioritize:

  • A. Immediate political equality
  • B. Vocational training and economic self-reliance
  • C. Militant protest against Jim Crow
  • D. Migration to northern cities
Show Answer

Answer: B — Washington argued Black Americans should accept segregation temporarily while building economic strength through industrial education.

Q9. The Nineteenth Amendment (1920) expanded suffrage by:

  • A. Granting citizenship to Native Americans
  • B. Prohibiting denial of the vote based on sex
  • C. Eliminating poll taxes
  • D. Lowering the voting age to 18
Show Answer

Answer: B — The Nineteenth Amendment guaranteed women the right to vote, the culmination of over seventy years of suffrage activism.

Q10. FDR's New Deal programs were primarily designed to address the Depression through:

  • A. Laissez-faire market correction
  • B. Federal relief, recovery, and reform measures
  • C. Austerity and balanced budgets
  • D. Military Keynesianism
Show Answer

Answer: B — The New Deal's three Rs used federal intervention to provide jobs, stabilize banks, and regulate markets.

Q11. The Truman Doctrine (1947) committed the United States to:

  • A. European reconstruction via Marshall Plan
  • B. Supporting free peoples resisting subjugation by armed minorities
  • C. Nuclear first-strike capability against the USSR
  • D. Collective security through NATO
Show Answer

Answer: B — Truman asked Congress for aid to Greece and Turkey, articulating broad policy of containing Soviet expansion.

Q12. Which 1954 Supreme Court case overturned Plessy v. Ferguson?

  • A. Engel v. Vitale
  • B. Brown v. Board of Education
  • C. Miranda v. Arizona
  • D. Gideon v. Wainwright
Show Answer

Answer: B — Brown unanimously ruled that racial segregation in public schools was unconstitutional.

Q13. The domino theory justified U.S. intervention in Vietnam by arguing that:

  • A. Vietnam possessed large oil reserves
  • B. Communist takeover of one nation would trigger neighboring nations to fall
  • C. North Vietnam had attacked U.S. ships
  • D. China directly controlled Hanoi
Show Answer

Answer: B — Eisenhower's domino theory held that if South Vietnam fell, surrounding nations would follow like falling dominoes.

Q14. The War Powers Resolution (1973) was passed primarily to:

  • A. Authorize the Vietnam War
  • B. Limit presidential ability to commit troops without congressional approval
  • C. Expand CIA covert operations
  • D. Create the Department of Homeland Security
Show Answer

Answer: B — Congress passed the War Powers Act over Nixon's veto to reassert legislative authority over military commitments.

Q15. Reagan's economic policy centered on:

  • A. Increasing corporate taxes to fund social programs
  • B. Tax cuts, deregulation, and reduced domestic spending
  • C. Expanding welfare programs to stimulate demand
  • D. Nationalizing key industries
Show Answer

Answer: B — Supply-side economics emphasized cutting the top marginal tax rate, deregulation, and reducing social spending.

Q16. Which event most directly triggered U.S. entry into World War I?

  • A. Sinking of the Lusitania
  • B. Germany's unrestricted submarine warfare resumption and the Zimmermann Telegram
  • C. Assassination of Franz Ferdinand
  • D. Austrian invasion of Serbia
Show Answer

Answer: B — Germany's 1917 resumption of unrestricted submarine warfare and the Zimmermann Telegram pushed Wilson to ask Congress for war.

Q17. The Harlem Renaissance was significant primarily because it:

  • A. Led directly to the Civil Rights Act
  • B. Produced a cultural flowering affirming Black identity and challenging racist stereotypes
  • C. Established historically Black colleges
  • D. Created the NAACP
Show Answer

Answer: B — The Harlem Renaissance produced an explosion of Black artistic and literary achievement that challenged racist caricatures.

Q18. The GI Bill (1944) most significantly contributed to:

  • A. Desegregation of the armed forces
  • B. Expansion of the white middle class through subsidized education and mortgages
  • C. Creation of the interstate highway system
  • D. Growth of labor union membership
Show Answer

Answer: B — The GI Bill provided veterans with tuition, low-interest mortgages, and benefits, fueling suburban growth and middle-class expansion.

Q19. The Seneca Falls Convention (1848) was significant primarily because it:

  • A. Launched the abolitionist movement
  • B. Produced the Declaration of Sentiments demanding women's equal rights
  • C. Founded the Republican Party
  • D. Led immediately to women's suffrage
Show Answer

Answer: B — The convention issued the Declaration of Sentiments modeled on the Declaration of Independence, launching the formal women's rights movement.

Q20. Which factor most significantly explains the failure of Reconstruction (1865-1877)?

  • A. Radical Republican indifference to Black rights
  • B. Northern war fatigue, Southern white resistance, and withdrawal of federal enforcement
  • C. Freedmen's unwillingness to participate in politics
  • D. Supreme Court support for Southern governments
Show Answer

Answer: B — The Compromise of 1877 ended federal enforcement; Northern public tired of effort; Southern terror campaigns suppressed Black political participation.

Free Response Questions

FRQ 1 (DBQ)

Evaluate the extent to which the Progressive Era (1890-1920) successfully reformed American democracy. In your essay: make a historically defensible thesis, provide contextualization, use at least 6 documents with HAPP sourcing for at least 3, and demonstrate complex understanding.
View Model Answer
THESIS: The Progressive Era achieved significant but incomplete democratic reforms—successfully curbing corporate power and expanding political participation, yet failing to extend these gains to African Americans. CONTEXTUALIZATION: The Progressive Era emerged from the Gilded Age's crises—industrial monopolies, urban machine corruption, and economic distress from the Populist movement (1890s). Progressives channeled pressure for reform through government rather than revolution. POLITICAL REFORMS: The 17th Amendment (1913) established direct election of senators. Initiative, referendum, and recall gave citizens direct lawmaking power. The Australian ballot reduced machine manipulation. (HAPP: A muckraker exposé—written by investigative journalist for middle-class reform audience—inflamed sentiment for reform.) ECONOMIC REGULATION: TR's enforcement of Sherman Antitrust Act (dissolution of Northern Securities, 1904), Clayton Antitrust Act (1914), Pure Food and Drug Act (1906), and Federal Reserve Act (1913) demonstrated government could regulate capitalism without abolishing it. LIMITS: African Americans were almost entirely excluded. Progressive Era saw peak Jim Crow, mass disenfranchisement, and lynching. Wilson resegregated the federal bureaucracy. Women gained suffrage only in 1920. COMPLEXITY: Many Progressives supported eugenics and immigration restriction—revealing how 'reform' could simultaneously democratize and restrict. The era created the regulatory state but encoded racial exclusion into it. CONCLUSION: The Progressive Era democratized political processes and regulated industrial capitalism, but reforms were racially bounded—a partial rather than complete democratic transformation.

FRQ 2 (LEQ)

Evaluate the relative importance of economic factors versus political factors in causing the Civil War. Develop an argument using specific evidence.
View Model Answer
THESIS: While sectional economic differences over slavery created irreconcilable interests, it was the failure of political institutions—particularly the collapse of the Second Party System—that transformed economic conflict into armed secession. CONTEXTUALIZATION: Four decades of sectional tension over slavery's expansion (Missouri Compromise 1820, Compromise of 1850, Kansas-Nebraska Act 1854) attempted political solutions to fundamentally economic disagreements. ECONOMIC ARGUMENT: The Southern slave economy required expansion—soil exhaustion demanded new cotton lands; slave states needed new slave states to maintain Senate parity. Northern free labor ideology viewed slave-labor competition as existential. POLITICAL ARGUMENT: Kansas-Nebraska Act destroyed the Whig Party and created Republicans on a sectional platform. 'Bleeding Kansas' made compromise seem impossible. John Brown's Harper's Ferry raid terrified the South. Lincoln's election—without a single Southern electoral vote—demonstrated the South had permanently lost national political power. COMPLEXITY: These factors were inseparable. However, slavery had existed 250 years without producing civil war; it was the specific political failures of 1854-1860 that made war the outcome rather than continued uneasy coexistence. CONCLUSION: Economic structures made conflict likely; political collapse made it inevitable.

FRQ 3 (SAQ)

a) Briefly explain ONE way the New Deal changed the relationship between the federal government and American citizens. b) Briefly explain ONE limitation of the New Deal. c) Briefly explain ONE way the New Deal's legacy shaped post-WWII American politics.
View Model Answer
a) The New Deal expanded the federal government's responsibility for citizens' economic welfare. Social Security (1935) created a permanent federal safety net—establishing the principle that government bore responsibility for protecting workers against old age and poverty, a radical departure from laissez-faire precedent. b) The New Deal largely excluded African Americans. Agricultural and domestic workers—disproportionately Black—were excluded from Social Security and the NLRA at the insistence of Southern Democratic congressmen. The FHA's redlining denied Black families access to subsidized mortgages, entrenching residential segregation. c) The New Deal created the modern Democratic coalition—urban workers, labor unions, ethnic minorities, and liberals—that dominated American politics until the 1960s. It also established the political precedent that the federal government had permanent welfare responsibilities. Reagan's 1980 victory was defined partly by running against this New Deal state, showing how it remained the defining axis of American political debate for fifty years.
