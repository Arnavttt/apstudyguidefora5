Exam Day Tips

✓

Use the HAPP-Y framework for every document: Historical situation, Audience, Purpose, Point of view, explain Why it matters.

✓

LEQ time periods: c.1200–1450, 1450–1750, 1750–1900, 1900–present—know the boundaries.

✓

For complexity, try comparing world regions rather than just one country's history.

✓

Avoid Eurocentrism: include Asia, Africa, and the Americas in every essay you write.

Multiple Choice (20 Questions)

Q1. The Columbian Exchange most directly resulted in:

  • A. European adoption of Islam
  • B. Population decline in the Americas due to disease
  • C. Immediate industrialization in Africa
  • D. Chinese dominance of Atlantic trade
Show Answer

Answer: B — European diseases like smallpox devastated indigenous Americans who lacked immunity, killing an estimated 50-90% in some regions.

Q2. The Atlantic slave trade's primary economic function was to:

  • A. Provide soldiers for European armies
  • B. Supply labor for New World plantation agriculture
  • C. Enable African states to industrialize
  • D. Fund the Protestant Reformation
Show Answer

Answer: B — The transatlantic slave trade forced ~12 million Africans to the Americas primarily to labor on sugar, tobacco, and cotton plantations.

Q3. Which factor most enabled European imperialism in Africa during the 19th century?

  • A. African political disunity alone
  • B. Technological advantages including quinine, railways, and repeating rifles
  • C. African invitations for European trade
  • D. European population surplus requiring emigration
Show Answer

Answer: B — Quinine (malaria prevention), steam power, railways, and Maxim guns gave Europeans decisive military and logistical advantages.

Q4. The Meiji Restoration (1868) in Japan was primarily a response to:

  • A. Chinese military invasion
  • B. Western imperial pressure and the threat of colonization
  • C. A peasant revolt against the Tokugawa shogunate
  • D. Economic depression
Show Answer

Answer: B — Perry's 'Black Ships' (1853) forced Japan open; reformers ended the shogunate and modernized Japan on Western lines to avoid colonization.

Q5. The Haitian Revolution (1791-1804) was historically significant primarily because:

  • A. It was the first successful slave revolution creating an independent Black republic
  • B. It led directly to Latin American independence
  • C. Napoleon used it to justify the Louisiana Purchase
  • D. It inspired the French Revolution
Show Answer

Answer: A — Haiti became the first and only nation established by a successful enslaved people's revolution, terrifying slaveholders throughout the Atlantic world.

Q6. Which best characterizes the economic relationship between colonies and metropoles under 19th-century imperialism?

  • A. Mutual benefit through free trade
  • B. Colonies supplying raw materials while purchasing manufactured goods
  • C. Equal industrial partnership
  • D. Colonial self-sufficiency encouraged by European powers
Show Answer

Answer: B — Imperial economies extracted raw materials from colonies and sold manufactured goods back—classic dependency relationships.

Q7. The Green Revolution of the mid-20th century primarily addressed:

  • A. Environmental pollution from industrialization
  • B. Food insecurity through high-yield crop varieties and agricultural technology
  • C. Political revolutions in developing nations
  • D. Deforestation in tropical regions
Show Answer

Answer: B — The Green Revolution introduced high-yield wheat and rice varieties, irrigation, and fertilizers, dramatically increasing food production in South Asia and Latin America.

Q8. The Non-Aligned Movement during the Cold War represented:

  • A. Nations supporting the Soviet bloc
  • B. Developing nations refusing alignment with either superpower
  • C. European neutral nations
  • D. The UN Security Council
Show Answer

Answer: B — Led by Nehru, Nasser, and Tito, NAM sought to avoid Cold War entanglement and represent a 'third world' alternative to both superpowers.

Q9. Which development most directly caused the collapse of the Mongol Empire?

  • A. Military defeat by the Ottoman Empire
  • B. Overextension, succession conflicts, and the Black Death
  • C. Chinese resistance under the Ming dynasty
  • D. European Crusade armies
Show Answer

Answer: B — The empire fragmented due to succession disputes and the Black Death (which spread along Mongol trade routes) devastated populations across Eurasia.

Q10. The Silk Roads were significant primarily because they facilitated:

  • A. Exclusively the trade of silk between China and Rome
  • B. The exchange of goods, diseases, ideas, and religions across Eurasia
  • C. Chinese political domination of Central Asia
  • D. Annual tribute payments to the Han Dynasty
Show Answer

Answer: B — The Silk Roads connected China to the Mediterranean, transmitting Buddhism, Islam, the Black Death, gunpowder, and paper.

Q11. The Berlin Conference (1884-85) is historically significant because:

  • A. It ended World War I
  • B. European powers partitioned Africa without African input
  • C. It established the League of Nations
  • D. Germany was demilitarized
Show Answer

Answer: B — European powers divided Africa into colonial territories, ignoring existing African political boundaries and sovereignty.

Q12. Gandhi's satyagraha strategy proved effective primarily because:

  • A. The British respected Indian religious traditions
  • B. It exposed British moral contradictions and mobilized international opinion
  • C. Indian military forces backed the protesters
  • D. The British feared economic retaliation
Show Answer

Answer: B — Nonviolence forced Britain to violently suppress peaceful protesters in view of world audiences, undermining Britain's self-image as a civilizing force.

Q13. The primary cause of World War I was:

  • A. German aggression alone
  • B. A combination of militarism, alliances, imperialism, and nationalism ignited by assassination
  • C. Austro-Hungarian desire to conquer Serbia
  • D. The assassination of Franz Ferdinand alone
Show Answer

Answer: B — The MAIN framework created conditions where a regional crisis escalated to world war; the assassination was a trigger, not the cause.

Q14. The Great Leap Forward (1958-62) in China primarily resulted in:

  • A. Rapid industrialization and improved living standards
  • B. Mass famine killing 15-45 million people
  • C. Successful collectivization of agriculture
  • D. Military conquest of Taiwan
Show Answer

Answer: B — Mao's forced collectivization and unrealistic industrial targets caused catastrophic agricultural failure and one of history's worst famines.

Q15. Why is the Ottoman Empire called 'the sick man of Europe' by the 19th century?

  • A. Military defeats and territorial losses to Russia and Balkan nationalists
  • B. Economic depression from the Great Drought
  • C. European colonization of Anatolia
  • D. Conversion of the population to Christianity
Show Answer

Answer: A — The Ottomans suffered serial military defeats, lost Balkan territories to nationalist movements, and became financially dependent on European creditors.

Q16. The Rwandan Genocide (1994) was most directly caused by:

  • A. Cold War proxy conflict
  • B. Colonial-era ethnic categorization exploited by Hutu extremists after Habyarimana's assassination
  • C. Economic competition over land
  • D. Long-standing tribal warfare dating to the 15th century
Show Answer

Answer: B — Belgian colonial rule hardened Hutu/Tutsi distinctions; extremist propaganda incited the genocide after the president's assassination.

Q17. The printing press (c.1450) most significantly contributed to:

  • A. The decline of European literacy
  • B. The Protestant Reformation and rapid spread of new ideas
  • C. Ottoman conquest of Constantinople
  • D. Portuguese maritime exploration
Show Answer

Answer: B — Gutenberg's press enabled mass production of vernacular texts including Luther's 95 Theses, making the Reformation's spread far more rapid than prior reform movements.

Q18. Decolonization after WWII was primarily driven by:

  • A. European generosity
  • B. Combination of nationalist movements, economic exhaustion of imperial powers, and Cold War pressure
  • C. UN military intervention
  • D. American military liberation of colonies
Show Answer

Answer: B — WWII discredited racial hierarchy; colonial peoples demanded independence; superpowers rhetorically opposed colonialism; nationalist movements had matured.

Q19. The primary significance of the trans-Saharan trade network was:

  • A. Transmitting Islam and connecting sub-Saharan Africa to Mediterranean economies
  • B. Supplying Rome with slaves
  • C. Allowing Mongol control of North Africa
  • D. Creating the Swahili city-states
Show Answer

Answer: A — The trans-Saharan routes carried gold and salt while transmitting Islam southward and connecting West African kingdoms to Mediterranean markets.

Q20. Which most accurately characterizes the relationship between capitalism and colonialism?

  • A. Colonialism preceded and enabled capital accumulation funding European industrialization
  • B. They were separate and unrelated phenomena
  • C. Colonialism hurt European economies
  • D. Industrialization caused colonialism as a search for raw materials alone

Answer: A — Colonial extraction of wealth through slavery, tribute, and trade monopolies provided capital that financed European industrialization.

Free Response Questions

FRQ 1 (DBQ)

Evaluate the extent to which industrialization improved standards of living in 19th-century Europe. Address continuity AND change. Use at least 6 documents with HAPP sourcing for at least 3.
View Model Answer
THESIS: Industrialization produced contradictory effects: while it generated long-term improvements in living standards and life expectancy, the immediate 19th-century experience for the working class was characterized by dangerous conditions, urban poverty, and social dislocation. CONTEXTUALIZATION: Industrialization emerged from the Agricultural Revolution and Enlightenment emphasis on scientific progress. Britain's coal resources, navigable rivers, colonial markets, and enclosure movement (displacing peasants who became factory labor) made it the 'first industrial nation.' FOR IMPROVEMENT: By mid-century, industrial production lowered costs of textiles and basic goods. Real wages rose after 1850. Railways moved food cheaply. By 1900, life expectancy had risen significantly. The middle class expanded dramatically. AGAINST IMPROVEMENT: Engels' Condition of the Working Class in England (1845) documented Manchester's slums—open sewers, 16-hour workdays, child labor in mines. Parliamentary testimony (Coal Mines Commission, 1842) showed children as young as 5 hauling coal. Urban mortality exceeded rural rates; cholera epidemics hit crowded industrial cities. (HAPP: Engels wrote as a committed socialist to persuade reformers—but his documentary evidence was corroborated by parliamentary investigations.) CONTINUITY/CHANGE: The transition brought new forms of suffering. Pre-industrial rural poverty was harsh but embedded in community; industrial urban poverty was atomized, anonymous, and subject to boom-bust cycles. COMPLEXITY: Periodize—before 1850 vs. after; differentiate by class (owners vs. workers), gender (women's factory labor changed family structures), and region (industrialized north vs. agricultural south).

FRQ 2 (LEQ)

Evaluate the extent to which the Mongol conquests had a negative impact on the regions they conquered in the period 1206-1368.
View Model Answer
THESIS: While the Mongol conquests caused catastrophic short-term destruction—killing millions and destroying cities—their long-term impact was more complex: Pax Mongolica enabled unprecedented Eurasian connectivity, trade, and cultural exchange. CONTEXTUALIZATION: Chinggis Khan unified Mongol tribes in 1206 and launched the largest contiguous land empire in history. Mongol military success rested on cavalry tactics, psychological terror, and sophisticated siege warfare. DESTRUCTIVE IMPACT: Baghdad's 1258 sack killed an estimated 100,000-800,000 people and destroyed the Abbasid Caliphate, including the House of Wisdom's libraries. Nishapur and Merv were destroyed almost completely. Agricultural infrastructure in Persia and Iraq was damaged so severely some regions never recovered. CONSTRUCTIVE IMPACT: The Pax Mongolica enabled secure trade along the Silk Roads. Marco Polo's journey and Ibn Battuta's travels were possible because of Mongol protection. Yuan Dynasty patronage of Chinese arts and Ilkhanate facilitation of Persian-Chinese exchange produced cultural synthesis. COMPLEXITY: The Black Death traveled along the very trade routes Mongol conquest reopened—demonstrating how the same connectivity enabling cultural exchange also enabled pandemic. The Mongol legacy is simultaneously constructive and catastrophic, depending on time period examined.

FRQ 3 (SAQ)

a) Briefly explain ONE way Atlantic trade networks changed African societies in the period 1500-1800. b) Briefly explain ONE way Atlantic trade networks changed European societies in the same period. c) Briefly explain ONE continuity in African political structures despite the slave trade's disruption.
View Model Answer
a) The Atlantic slave trade militarized African political structures. Coastal kingdoms like Dahomey became dependent on slave raiding for firearms, creating a tragic cycle: they needed guns to capture slaves, and captured slaves to trade for guns. This intensified internal African warfare, depopulated some interior regions, and shifted power toward coastal intermediary states. b) The Atlantic trade transformed European consumer culture and diets. Sugar, tobacco, coffee, chocolate, and tea—all colonial products—became mass consumption items in Europe by 1700. This 'consumer revolution' stimulated demand, supported plantation slavery, and contributed to proto-industrial production. The profits of Atlantic trade contributed to capital accumulation in Bristol, Liverpool, Amsterdam, and Lisbon. c) Despite massive disruption, many African political institutions maintained their internal structures and sovereignty. The Kingdom of Kongo continued to function as a centralized monarchical state, maintain diplomatic relations with European powers, and convert selectively to Christianity as a tool of statecraft—using engagement with the Atlantic world on its own terms rather than simply succumbing to it.
