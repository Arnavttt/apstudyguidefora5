AP® Free-Response Habits

Writing Response Toolkit

Move from claim to quoted/paraphrased evidence to commentary that explains meaning or rhetorical effect.

Analyze

Explain how the writer or text creates meaning, persuasion, tone, or complexity.

Develop

Build a defensible line of reasoning rather than listing examples.

Support

Choose evidence that directly proves the claim.

Synthesize

Put sources in conversation instead of summarizing them one by one.

Watch for: Device naming is not analysis; always say what the detail does for the argument, speaker, or theme.

AP® Exam-Day Strategy

Have a plan before the timer starts

Use this course bank to rehearse pacing, then carry a simple pass system into the official exam. For this course, keep watching for literary evidence, interpretation, and line-level commentary and command verbs like Analyze, Develop, Support.

First pass

Answer the clear questions first, mark uncertainty, and keep moving so one hard prompt does not steal the section.

Evidence pass

Return to marked items and check that every response points to evidence, a setup, a graph, a source, code behavior, or a mechanism.

Final check

Use remaining time to catch missing units, labels, thesis/claim language, command verbs, and unsupported explanations.

Q2
A literary motif differs from a theme in that a motif is
  • A) the climax of the plot
  • B) a recurring image, symbol, or situation that reinforces meaning, while a theme is the central idea or message

  • D) the narrator's point of view
B is correct. a recurring image, symbol, or situation that reinforces meaning, while a theme is the central idea or message

  • C) in the middle of the action, with background filled in later through flashback or exposition

C is correct. in the middle of the action, with background filled in later through flashback or exposition
